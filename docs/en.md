## Floors

<details>
<summary><strong>Sticky floors after mopping</strong></summary>

Cleaner residue and dirty mops can leave a sticky film. Remove loose dirt first and use the dose on the label.

**Material:** Ceramic / porcelain  
**Tools:** Microfiber mop, two water containers, suitable floor cleaner.

1. Sweep or vacuum grit before mopping.
2. Use a damp mop. Replace dirty water and keep kitchen and bathroom mops separate.
3. Remove residue with clean water if the label allows, then dry.

> **Limits:** Do not add extra cleaner for fragrance. Dry slippery floors before walking on them.

Origin: photo 1, entries 3-4, 19, 22. Steps edited.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Dull granite or marble</strong></summary>

Identify the material: natural stone differs from tiles sold as granite. Soap cannot repair an etched finish.

**Material:** Natural stone  
**Tools:** Soft cloth, pH-neutral stone cleaner, clean water.

1. Check the material specification with the installer or manufacturer.
2. Remove loose dirt, use a stone-safe product as labeled, rinse and dry.
3. If dullness or scratches remain, ask a stone specialist to assess it.

> **Limits:** Avoid vinegar, lemon, acidic descalers and abrasive powders on natural stone.

Origin: photo 1, entries 1-18. Steps edited.

<details>
<summary><strong>Caring for vinyl and wood floors</strong></summary>

Water entering joints can damage flooring. Use a barely damp mop and a product approved for the finish.

**Material:** Vinyl / finished wood  
**Tools:** Soft broom, damp mop, manufacturer-approved cleaner.

1. Remove dust and grit without dragging them across the surface.
2. For vinyl, follow neutral-cleaner guidance. For wood, follow its specific care instructions.
3. Wipe spills promptly and keep joints dry.

> **Limits:** Do not flood, steam-mop or machine-polish without manufacturer approval.

Origin: photo 2, entries 44-47, 51. Steps edited.

<details>
<summary><strong>Dark tile grout</strong></summary>

Grout needs gentle brushing. Recurring discoloration can be linked to moisture or damaged grout.

**Material:** Cement / epoxy grout  
**Tools:** Nylon brush, grout-compatible cleaner.

1. Identify the grout and surrounding tile.
2. Apply compatible cleaner as labeled, brush gently and rinse.
3. Dry the area. Repair cracked or loose grout.

> **Limits:** Avoid wire brushes and strong acids. Bleach cannot fix the source of moisture.

Origin: photo 2, entries 58, 62. Steps edited.

<details>
<summary><strong>Oil or soap spills</strong></summary>

Secure the slippery area first. Absorb the spill before mopping so it does not spread.

**Material:** Sealed hard floors  
**Tools:** Absorbent material, dustpan, floor-compatible cleaner.

1. Keep people away. Absorb with paper towels or suitable absorbent material.
2. Remove the residue, then clean with a suitable labeled product.
3. Rinse if required and dry until the floor is no longer slippery.

> **Limits:** Do not pour oil down drains or boiling water onto floors.

Origin: photo 1, entries 24-26. Steps edited.

<details>
<summary><strong>Cement residue after renovation</strong></summary>

Cement removers must match the surface. Test first because acidic products can damage stone, metal and grout.

**Material:** Post-renovation tile  
**Tools:** Material care guide, plastic scraper if permitted.

1. Ask the installer to confirm tile type and grout curing time.
2. Carefully lift loose residue without scratching.
3. For stubborn residue, use a post-renovation cleaner familiar with the material.

> **Limits:** Do not reach straight for toilet cleaner, blades or strong acid.

Origin: photo 1, entries 1, 21. Steps edited.

## Bathroom

<details>
<summary><strong>Water marks on taps</strong></summary>

Wipe and dry first. Black, gold and chrome finishes have different care requirements.

**Material:** Coated taps  
**Tools:** Soft cloth, approved mild soap.

1. Check the finish care guide before choosing a cleaner.
2. Wipe with a damp cloth and mild soap if approved.
3. Remove residue with a clean damp cloth and dry after use.

> **Limits:** Avoid aluminum foil, abrasive pads and vinegar soaks on decorative finishes.

Origin: photo 3, entries 71-77, 84-92. Steps edited.

<details>
<summary><strong>Bathroom limescale</strong></summary>

Distinguish mineral deposits, soap residue and surface damage. Choose products based on the tile material.

**Material:** Ceramic / porcelain  
**Tools:** Gloves, nylon brush, compatible descaler.

1. Ventilate. Remove soap residue with a compatible cleaner.
2. If deposits remain, use one manufacturer-approved descaler for the labeled contact time.
3. Rinse thoroughly and remove water with a squeegee.

> **Limits:** Do not use descaler on marble. Never mix or layer acidic products with bleach.

Origin: photo 1, entries 28-43. Steps edited.

<details>
<summary><strong>Toilet stains</strong></summary>

Keep toilet tools separate. Choose one cleaner suitable for the porcelain and your sanitation system.

**Material:** Sanitary porcelain  
**Tools:** Toilet brush, gloves, one toilet cleaner.

1. Ventilate and read the product label.
2. Apply as directed, wait for the labeled time, brush and flush.
3. For plastic seats, use a product approved by the seat manufacturer.

> **Limits:** Never mix toilet cleaner and bleach. Do not pour concentrated or excessive acid into septic systems.

Origin: photo 4, entries 114-127. Steps edited.

<details>
<summary><strong>Mold on bathroom silicone</strong></summary>

Mold embedded in sealant may not wipe away. Check leaks and the condition of the joint.

**Material:** Silicone sealant  
**Tools:** Gloves, cloth, material-compatible cleaner.

1. Fix leaks and help the area dry with ventilation.
2. Clean a small area according to product directions and dry it.
3. If silicone stays stained, lifts or cracks, arrange replacement after the area dries.

> **Limits:** Extensive, recurring or hidden mold needs professional assessment. Do not paint over it.

Origin: photo 3, entries 91, 98. Steps edited.

<details>
<summary><strong>Blocked or smelly drains</strong></summary>

Start with the strainer and water trap. Persistent odor can indicate a plumbing issue, not a need for fragrance.

**Material:** Sink / floor drains  
**Tools:** Gloves, strainer cleaning tool.

1. Remove accessible hair and food debris from the strainer.
2. For rarely used drains, check the water trap for that plumbing system.
3. Call a plumber if flow stays slow, leaks appear or odor returns.

> **Limits:** If a chemical drain opener was used, do not plunge or add another product. Tell the plumber.

Origin: photo 4, entries 112-113. Steps edited.

<details>
<summary><strong>Streaky glass and mirrors</strong></summary>

A little liquid and a clean cloth are usually enough. Coated glass needs its manufacturer’s care instructions.

**Material:** Glass / mirrors  
**Tools:** Glass microfiber cloth, compatible glass cleaner.

1. Remove dust, then lightly dampen the cloth with cleaner.
2. Wipe evenly without soaking mirror edges or backing.
3. Dry with a clean part of the cloth. Squeegee shower glass after use.

> **Limits:** Avoid pumice, sandpaper and blades on coated glass. Do not use glass cleaner on screens.

Origin: photo 4, entries 128-136. Steps edited.

## Kitchen

<details>
<summary><strong>Dull kitchen sink</strong></summary>

Clean and dry water residue. Stainless steel and granite composite need different products.

**Material:** Stainless / composite  
**Tools:** Soft sponge, dish soap, dry cloth.

1. Remove food scraps and clean the strainer.
2. Wash with an approved soap. Follow the grain on stainless steel.
3. Rinse and dry. For composite sinks, check the brand’s care guide.

> **Limits:** Do not treat all black sinks alike. Avoid steel wool and unapproved abrasives.

Origin: photo 4, entries 100-113. Steps edited.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Greasy cooktop</strong></summary>

Clean when cool. Choose a product made for the cooktop surface, especially induction glass and stainless steel.

**Material:** Gas / induction cooktop  
**Tools:** Cloth, soft sponge, manufacturer-approved cleaner.

1. Switch off and let cool. Disconnect power as instructed by the manual.
2. Remove food debris and clean removable parts according to the manual.
3. Wipe away residue and dry. Reassemble only when all parts are dry.

> **Limits:** Do not flood burners, spray gas ports or scrub glass with foil.

Origin: photo 5, entries 163-175. Steps edited.

<details>
<summary><strong>Burnt cookware</strong></summary>

Check the material and coating before scrubbing. Stainless care does not automatically suit nonstick cookware.

**Material:** Stainless / nonstick  
**Tools:** Dish soap, soft sponge, warm water.

1. Let the pan cool to avoid sudden temperature changes.
2. Soak approved parts in warm soapy water.
3. Lift residue with a soft sponge. Follow the pan’s specific care guide if it remains.

> **Limits:** Avoid wire brushes and melamine sponges on nonstick coatings. Assess peeling cookware for replacement.

<summary><strong>Dirty ovens and air fryers</strong></summary>

Remove crumbs and grease when cool. Oven chemicals are not always suitable for air fryers.

**Material:** Heating appliances  
**Tools:** Appliance manual, soft sponge, soap for washable parts.

1. Unplug where applicable and let cool.
2. Wash approved trays or baskets and wipe the cavity as instructed.
3. Dry thoroughly. Use a self-clean cycle only as instructed.

> **Limits:** Do not spray heating elements, fans or connectors. Never mix cleaners.

<summary><strong>Fridge odor after a power cut</strong></summary>

Find the odor source and assess food safety first. Fragrance cannot make spoiled food safe.

**Material:** Refrigerator interior  
**Tools:** Food containers, soft cloth, manual-approved mild soap.

1. Move safe food to cold storage. Discard unsafe food.
2. Switch off as instructed. Let removable shelves reach room temperature before washing.
3. Wash, rinse and dry shelves and door seals before reassembly.

> **Limits:** Do not expose cold glass to hot water or spray electrical parts. Persistent odor needs servicing.

<summary><strong>Smelly washing machine</strong></summary>

Care for detergent residue, drawers and door seals. Use the cleaning cycle specified for the model.

**Material:** Washing machine  
**Tools:** Machine manual, cloth, approved drum cleaner.

1. Clean the drawer and seal as instructed. Remove visible debris.
2. Run the empty drum-clean cycle with the recommended product.
3. Dry seal folds. Allow ventilation only when safe from children.

> **Limits:** Never add sulfuric acid or mix vinegar and bleach. Open filters as instructed to avoid flooding.

<details>
<summary><strong>Baby bottles and feeding items</strong></summary>

Take apart, wash, rinse and air-dry after use. Clean small components separately.

**Material:** Infant feeding items  
**Tools:** Dedicated basin and brush, soap, safe water.

1. Wash hands. Disassemble the bottle and rinse under running water.
2. Wash in a dedicated basin with a dedicated brush, then rinse.
3. Air-dry fully. Sanitize according to the baby’s needs and manufacturer instructions.

> **Limits:** Do not use adhesive solvents, household descalers or denture tablets without manufacturer approval. Replace moldy parts that cannot be cleaned.

<details>
<summary><strong>Smelly bottles and dark seals</strong></summary>

Odor often remains in the lid and seal. Remove only components designed to come apart.

**Material:** Drinking bottles / silicone  
**Tools:** Bottle brush, dish soap, clean water.

1. Remove seals as instructed and inspect for cracks or mold.
2. Wash the bottle, lid and crevices with soap and rinse thoroughly.
3. Dry open before reassembling. Replace damaged or persistently moldy seals.

> **Limits:** Do not soak electronic lids. Use bottle-cleaning tablets only if the brand permits them.

<summary><strong>Blender and juicer residue</strong></summary>

Separate washable parts from the motor. Remove food trapped in crevices instead of masking its smell.

**Material:** Plastic / glass / metal  
**Tools:** Soft brush, dish soap, appliance manual.

1. Unplug and disassemble approved parts.
2. Wash the jar and parts as instructed; avoid blade edges.
3. Rinse and dry. Wipe the motor unit with a well-wrung cloth.

> **Limits:** Do not immerse the motor or spray alcohol inside it.

## Fabric & furniture

<details>
<summary><strong>Stained sofa</strong></summary>

Check the fabric care code. Blot without rubbing to avoid spreading the stain or damaging fibers.

**Material:** Upholstery  
**Tools:** Clean white cloth, cleaner matching the fabric care code.

1. Lift solids and blot liquids from the edge toward the center.
2. Check the sofa label and test the product in a hidden area.
3. Use minimal liquid if allowed. Dry through the padding before use.

> **Limits:** Do not spray alcohol or steam every type of sofa. Consider a specialist for deep stains or urine odor.

Origin: photo 7, entries 218-223, 230-231. Steps edited.

[American Cleaning Institute: stain removal](https://www.cleaninginstitute.org/cleaning-tips/clothes/stain-removal-guide)

<details>
<summary><strong>Liquid on a mattress</strong></summary>

Blot promptly and prevent trapped moisture. Wet inner foam needs thorough drying.

**Material:** Mattress / foam  
**Tools:** Clean towels, ventilation, mattress care guide.

1. Remove bedding and press clean towels onto the liquid without rubbing.
2. Follow the manufacturer’s stain method with as little liquid as possible.
3. Dry fully before replacing bedding. Contact a specialist if the core is wet.

> **Limits:** Do not soak a mattress or use it while damp. Flood-soaked mattresses need special assessment.

Origin: photo 9, entries 298-301. Steps edited.

[EPA: mold cleanup at home](https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-cleanup-your-home)

<details>
<summary><strong>Dirty leather bags and shoes</strong></summary>

Leather, suede and synthetic materials need different care. Solvents can strip color or protective finishes.

**Material:** Leather / synthetic  
**Tools:** Soft cloth, brand care guide.

1. Identify the material and finish from the label.
2. Remove dust with an appropriate cloth or brush. Test care products in a hidden area.
3. Dry in a ventilated place away from direct heat.

> **Limits:** Do not use vinegar, alcohol or adhesive remover as a general solution. Extensive mold needs a leather specialist.

Origin: photo 9, entries 290-297, 302-303. Steps edited.

<summary><strong>Musty laundry</strong></summary>

Check washing and drying. Extra fragrance does not fix damp fabric or detergent residue.

**Material:** Washable fabrics  
**Tools:** Fabric label, suitable detergent, ventilated drying space.

1. Sort laundry and follow the temperature and dose on the label.
2. Do not overfill the drum. Use the machine’s rinse cycle.
3. Remove promptly and space items apart until fully dry.

> **Limits:** Never mix chlorine bleach with other cleaners or fragrances. Check colorfastness before using stain removers.

<summary><strong>Blood, fruit or oil stains</strong></summary>

The first action depends on the stain. Follow the care label and avoid heat drying before treating it.

**Material:** Clothes / bedding  
**Tools:** Water, detergent, compatible stain remover.

1. Lift residue without rubbing it into fibers. Start blood stains with cold water.
2. Pretreat with a product suitable for the stain and fabric, then wash as labeled.
3. Inspect before heat drying. Repeat treatment as directed if needed.

> **Limits:** Do not assume one soap suits every stain. Dry-clean-only fabrics need specific care.

Origin: photo 8, entries 257, 263-264. Steps edited.

<summary><strong>Ink and nail polish on fabric</strong></summary>

Identify fibers and dyes before using a solvent. Strong liquids can fade or damage fabric.

**Material:** Dyed fabric  
**Tools:** Fabric label, white cloth, laundry specialist if needed.

1. Do not rub or heat the stain. Note the ink or polish type if known.
2. Check the label. Use only a compatible stain remover and test in a hidden area.
3. Take delicate or valuable fabric to a professional before trying solvents.

> **Limits:** 99.9% alcohol is not universal. Acetone damages some fibers and finishes.

<summary><strong>Pet urine indoors</strong></summary>

Locate and remove urine, then dry. Avoid masking it with fragrance or essential oils.

**Material:** Fabric / sealed floors  
**Tools:** Paper towels, surface-compatible pet urine cleaner.

1. Keep pets away and blot the liquid.
2. Use a urine-specific product as labeled for the surface. Patch-test fabric.
3. Dry fully before reuse. Contact a specialist for deep contamination.

## Other areas

<details>
<summary><strong>Dusty screens and keyboards</strong></summary>

Switch off and use a soft lint-free cloth. Screen care instructions matter more than general cleaning recipes.

**Material:** Electronics  
**Tools:** Lint-free cloth, device care guide.

1. Switch off and unplug as instructed.
2. Wipe dust with a clean cloth. If allowed, lightly dampen the cloth, not the screen.
3. Clean keyboard gaps without introducing liquid. Let dry before powering on.

> **Limits:** Avoid household glass cleaner, bleach or concentrated alcohol unless approved. Never spray directly.

<summary><strong>Dusty fans and exhausts</strong></summary>

Disconnect power before opening covers. Remove dust without wetting motors or wiring.

**Material:** Fans / exhausts  
**Tools:** Soft brush, vacuum, cloth, stable access equipment.

1. Isolate power at the correct source. Confirm blades have stopped.
2. Clean accessible covers and blades according to the manual.
3. Dry washable parts before reassembly. Use a technician for high installations.

> **Limits:** Do not stand on an unstable chair or spray water into the motor.

Origin: photo 8, entries 244-245. Steps edited.

<summary><strong>Moldy walls in rainy weather</strong></summary>

Fix leaks and moisture first. Cleaning stains alone will not prevent mold from returning.

**Material:** Walls / ceilings  
**Tools:** Appropriate protection, ventilation, professional help when needed.

1. Find the source: roof, pipe, seepage or condensation.
2. For small areas on hard surfaces, follow EPA guidance and use compatible products.
3. Dry fully. Moldy porous materials may need replacement.

> **Limits:** Consult a professional for mold larger than about 0.9 m², major water damage or contaminated water. Color does not identify mold type.

The surface determines adhesive removal. Solvents can strip paint, plastic and furniture finishes.

**Material:** Glass / furniture / plastic  
**Tools:** Cloth, plastic scraper if allowed, material care guide.

1. Peel slowly without lifting the surface finish.
2. Choose a material-specific adhesive remover and patch-test.
3. Lift residue and remove cleaner residue as labeled.

> **Limits:** Avoid blades on coated glass and concentrated alcohol on unapproved plastics or paint.

Keep contents and joints dry. Scrubbing can turn a matte finish shiny.

**Material:** Wood / laminate / paint  
**Tools:** Soft cloth, finish-compatible cleaner.

1. Empty the cabinet and check for leaks or damp walls behind it.
2. Dust first. Clean marks with a well-wrung cloth and approved product.
3. Dry joints before putting items back.

> **Limits:** Avoid melamine sponges on matte paint and excess water on MDF. Swollen wood needs repair.

<details>
<summary><strong>Algae on patios and paving</strong></summary>

Improve drainage and persistently wet shaded areas. Choose a method that does not erode joints or stone.

**Material:** Paving / outdoor stone  
**Tools:** Material-appropriate brush, slip-resistant footwear.

1. Restrict access because the area can be very slippery.
2. Brush with a suitable method and control runoff.
3. If using a pressure washer, follow surface-specific pressure and distance limits.

<a id="faq"></a>

## Common questions

<details>
<summary>Can I mix bleach with soap or vinegar?</summary>

No. Use one product as labeled. Bleach can react with other cleaners and release dangerous vapors. If disinfection is needed, clean first and follow the disinfectant label in a separate process.

[CDC: cleaning with bleach](https://www.cdc.gov/hygiene/about/cleaning-and-disinfecting-with-bleach.html)

</details>

<details>
<summary>Do vinegar and baking soda work better together?</summary>

Foaming does not prove cleaning performance. They react and neutralize each other. Choose a product suited to the soil and material; do not treat the mixture as a disinfectant.



</details>

<details>
<summary>Does the whole home need daily disinfection?</summary>

For routine care, start by removing dirt. Disinfect when needed, such as after specific contamination, using a surface-compatible product and its label directions. Fragrance does not measure disinfection.

[CDC: cleaning with bleach](https://www.cdc.gov/hygiene/about/cleaning-and-disinfecting-with-bleach.html)

</details>

<details>
<summary>Why does mold return in the rainy season?</summary>

The moisture source may remain. Check seepage, leaks and condensation. Clean suitable small areas according to guidance and dry them. Extensive mold or contaminated water needs professional assessment.

[EPA: mold cleanup at home](https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-cleanup-your-home)

</details>

<details>
<summary>Are granite tiles the same as natural granite?</summary>

Not necessarily. The trade name granite is often used for porcelain tiles. Check specifications or ask the installer. If the material is unknown, avoid acids and abrasives until identified.

[Natural Stone Institute: care and cleaning](https://www.naturalstoneinstitute.org/consumers/care/)

</details>

<details>
<summary>Are melamine sponges safe on everything?</summary>

No. Melamine foam is abrasive and can alter matte paint, nonstick coatings and other finishes. Follow the manufacturer and always test a hidden area.



</details>

<details>
<summary>How can I save water while cleaning?</summary>

Remove dry soil first, use a damp mop and turn off the tap while scrubbing. Still complete any label-required rinsing, especially on food-contact surfaces.



</details>

<details>
<summary>How should I clean around babies or pets?</summary>

Keep products in original containers out of reach. Keep babies and pets away during cleaning and follow label rinsing and re-entry instructions. Essential oils are not automatically safe or disinfectants.

[CDC: infant feeding items](https://www.cdc.gov/hygiene/about/clean-sanitize-store-infant-feeding-items.html)

</details>

<details>
<summary>Which cleaners are safe for septic systems?</summary>

Follow product labels and septic-system guidance. Do not pour solvents, oil, poisons or excessive chemicals down drains. Repeated blockages need a plumbing and tank inspection, not more chemicals.

[EPA: care for your septic system](https://www.epa.gov/septic/how-care-your-septic-system)

</details>

<details>
<summary>When should I stop and call a professional?</summary>

Stop if the surface discolors, its coating lifts, irritation occurs or the material is unknown. Call a professional for wet electrics, enclosed tanks, leaks, extensive mold and damaged stone.

[EPA: mold cleanup at home](https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-cleanup-your-home) | [CDC: safe cleaning after a disaster](https://www.cdc.gov/natural-disasters/safety/index.html)

</details>
